# CS 330 Computational Graphics and Visualization

This repository contains my final project for CS 330: Computational Graphics and Visualization. The project includes a completed 3D scene inspired by Alexej von Jawlensky’s *Still Life with Bottles and Fruit*, along with my Design Decisions document explaining the choices I made throughout development.

## How do I approach designing software?

This project helped me get more comfortable starting with a larger idea and breaking it into smaller, manageable pieces. Instead of looking at the reference image as one complicated scene, I separated it into basic shapes that could be built individually and then combined. That made it easier to plan the bottles, fruit, plate, and tabletop before worrying about textures, lighting, and camera movement.

My design process was iterative. I started with the basic geometry, then adjusted scale and placement before moving on to textures, materials, lighting, and navigation. I found that making one change at a time made it easier to see what worked and track down problems when something did not look right. That same approach can be applied to future projects by separating larger systems into smaller components and testing each part before adding more complexity.

## How do I approach developing programs?

One of the biggest development strategies I used in this course was incremental testing. I rarely tried to change several major parts of the scene at once. I would make a change, build the project, check the result, and then continue from there. This was especially useful when working with textures and lighting because small changes could have a noticeable effect on the final scene.

Iteration played a major role throughout the milestones. The project started with simple shapes and gradually became a complete scene as each module introduced new requirements. Along the way, I became more comfortable troubleshooting OpenGL code, adjusting transformations, working with materials and lighting, and organizing related tasks into separate functions. My approach has become more deliberate than it was at the beginning of the course. I now spend more time thinking about how pieces of a program fit together before making changes.

## How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?

Computational graphics gave me another way to think about how software can represent and interact with real-world systems. From an educational standpoint, the course strengthened my understanding of transformations, coordinate systems, rendering, user input, and how different parts of a graphics pipeline work together.

Professionally, I can see these skills connecting with my interest in automation, robotics, and programming. Visualization could be used for equipment simulations, robot workcells, process monitoring, training tools, or interfaces that make machine data easier to understand. Even if I do not work directly in computer graphics, the problem-solving and development process from this course applies to the type of technical work I want to pursue.
